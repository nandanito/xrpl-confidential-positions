# Upstream feedback register

Observations about XLS-96, its documentation, its reference implementation and its surrounding tooling, accumulated while building against it. Destined for the RippleX product and engineering teams working on privacy and confidentiality.

**This register is not `CLAIMS.md`.** That one governs whether *our* statements are true. This one records where *their* product could be better, and where it is already better than it looks. An item can appear in both — F-1 is a claim we might publish and a documentation bug they should fix — but the two lists answer different questions and drift apart quickly.

---

## The bar for anything in this register

**Higher than the publishing bar, not lower.** A blog post that overstates something gets corrected by a stranger. Feedback sent to the engineers who wrote the thing gets read by people who know the answer immediately, and one wrong item discredits the ten correct ones next to it. Every entry carries the same A–F grade and the same source citation as anything in `CLAIMS.md`, and anything ungraded stays in `Observed` until it is verified or dropped.

**No manufactured symmetry.** Positive entries are here because they record something specific and non-obvious — a design decision that is better than it appears, or an integrity property nobody has advertised. They are not here to soften the negative ones. If a positive entry could be written about any competent codebase, it is padding and does not belong.

**Public filing beats private memos.** A pull request that fixes four documentation errors is worth more than a document describing them, and it is checkable by anyone. Default to the public channel — a GitHub issue on `XRPLF/rippled`, `XRPLF/XRPL-Standards`, `XRPLF/xrpl-dev-portal` or `XRPLF/mpt-crypto`, or a documentation PR. ⚠ **The XLS-0096 discussion thread (#372) is LOCKED and is not a channel**, checked 2026-08-26. Use a direct conversation for things that genuinely do not fit a public channel: roadmap-shaped observations, prioritisation questions, or anything where the useful content is a judgement rather than a defect.

Checking the specification, the implementation and the documentation is not sufficient. Check the audit. **Read Appendices B, C and D as well as the 17 issues.** They hold code-quality, performance and API observations that are not among the 17, and the fix review does not track them; it mentions Appendix B once, about magic numbers.
