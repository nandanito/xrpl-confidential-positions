My understanding is right. It was vague that the Send arrow seems to leave from the Holder A column's Spending balance but visually points at nothing specific on B's side. On desktop the middle "Send" arrow floats between the columns, and the box below it ("The Send carries ...") is a text box in a neutral colour, so it reads as a third actor, not a note.
- Who can read what: the matrix rows (Anyone, A holder, The issuer, An auditor) against five columns. I understood issuer and auditor read everything and the public sees only the first three columns. Dashed cells = partial yes. I understood this only because of the caption; the dashed border is a weak cue.
- Facility: Bank (purple) funds the Dedicated Account (amount public), one Convert of the whole float, then Sends to Suppliers A, B, C (amounts hidden, who and when public), each enrolling publicly, then retirement in tranches via ConvertBack back to public balances. Hidden: limits, drawdown sizes, what each owes. I understood it. Vague: what a "float" is (never defined), what a Dedicated Account is beyond "ordinary XRPL account", and why suppliers are enrolled "with a small public payment" (the label says it, the reason is not given).
- Runs on a holder's machine: two lanes. Viewing: local web page, HTTP, read-only API, socket "Status only", wconf-signer. Transacting: facility driver, socket "Pair, then Prepare", wconf-signer (one per party), "the driver submits", Devnet. I understood that the signer holds keys and signs, and that the web page can only ask for Status. Vague: "Pair, then Prepare" are unexplained terms; "the facility sets fail_hard on its movements" is unexplained jargon; and the arrow direction between the signer and Devnet could be misread as the signer submitting (the text beside it says it does not).
