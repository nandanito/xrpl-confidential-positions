# REVIEW-063 leg C — the subagent's report, verbatim

Prompt: read exactly three PNG renders of the figures (figure 1, 2 and 3 at 1800 px wide)
and nothing else; for each, say what it says, how the arrows and groupings read, what the

FIGURE 1 ("Only a Send hides its amount. Who pays whom is always public.")
1. Says: tokens move from an ordinary public balance into a confidential pool (Convert), can be sent between holders with the amount hidden (Send), and move back out (ConvertBack). Convert and ConvertBack amounts are public. Observers always see sender, receiver, timing and the total confidential; the issuer (and an auditor, if named) can read every confidential balance. I understood it, though "visibly zero" is only explained in the footnote.
2. Arrows: "Convert" (left box to the confidential box) = holder moves a public holding in; "amount public" under it is clear. "Send" holder A to holder B = A pays B, amount hidden; clear. "ConvertBack" = confidential to public holding; clear. Issue: the two outer boxes are both titled "A public balance", so it can read as one balance or as two unrelated ones. The Convert and ConvertBack arrows enter and leave the big box's edge, not holder A / holder B specifically, so it is unclear whose balance moves. Nothing is mislabelled by grouping, but the "PUBLIC EVERYWHERE" and "THE ISSUER READS..." panels sit below with no link to the diagram, which is fine.
3. Colour: the dark thick border/left bar and dark Send arrow on the confidential box read as "the protected zone". That implies safe/private, which the box's own text only partly supports. The small grey "amount public" labels are low emphasis, so the public parts read as minor while the dark parts draw the eye.
4. Stronger than it should: the dark framing around "CONFIDENTIAL BALANCES" plus "amount hidden" visually promises privacy, while the issuer-reads caveat is in a plain grey box at the bottom. "each balance hidden, unless visibly zero" says "hidden" with no "from whom"; the issuer panel fixes this but is far from it. "No setting turns this off." is good and clear.
5. Legibility: fine. The grey "amount public" and "sender and receiver public" text is small and light but readable. The footnote is italic and light grey.
6. Fixes: change "each balance hidden, unless visibly zero" to "each balance hidden from the public, unless visibly zero". Change "amount hidden" to "amount hidden from observers". Rename the right box "A public balance" to "A public balance again" (as Figure 3 does) or add "(holder's)" to both. Define "visibly zero" in the footnote itself or inline.

FIGURE 2 ("The issuer reads every balance. Only the holder's keys can spend one.")
1. Says: a matrix of four parties (anyone, a holder, the issuer, an auditor if named) against five things. Everyone sees who pays whom and conversion amounts. Only holders (their own) and the issuer/auditor (every one) can read balances and Send amounts. Only a holder, with both keys, can spend; the issuer cannot spend but can destroy one by clawback or may freeze one.
2. Arrows: none. Grouping: row order puts "The issuer" accent-marked between "A holder" and "An auditor", which is fine. The auditor row looks like the issuer row without the emphasis, which is accurate.
3. Colour: dashed borders mean "partial yes" (the footer says so). The dark outline on the issuer's two "every one" cells and the dark bar on the issuer row mean "this is the point". Grey fill means "no". Possible mis-read: dashed for "their own" under a holder reads as "less certain/partial" when it is actually a full yes to their own data. The dashed "only whether it is visibly zero" for Anyone is genuinely partial. Using one dashed style for both is a mixed meaning. Grey "no" cells read as "greyed out/disabled" rather than a positive guarantee, which is slightly weak for the headline "no" on Anyone/Can spend.
4. Stronger than it should: the "no" in "Can spend a balance" for the issuer and auditor is stated flatly, and the "no; can destroy one by clawback, may freeze one" is small grey text that could be skimmed past. "A Send's amount: no" for Anyone could suggest total privacy, though the dark issuer cells counter it. The title carries the right balance.
5. Legibility: good. The three-line issuer spend cell ("no; can destroy / one by clawback, / may freeze one") is the densest and is greyed, so it is the least readable cell yet the most important caveat.
6. Fixes: make the issuer spend cell the same text weight/colour as other cells (not greyed), or lead with "no, but". Use a different style for "their own" cells (solid) than for the true partials. Consider "A holder's balance" to "A holder's balance (the holder's own)" is redundant; leave it. Footer: "Dashed: a partial yes." is good; add "Grey: no."

FIGURE 3 ("It hides how much each supplier draws and owes, not who or when.")
1. Says: a bank funds a Dedicated Account (public amount), which does one Convert into a confidential float. From the float, Sends (drawdowns and repayments, in both directions) go to Suppliers A, B and C; amounts are hidden but who and when are public. At the end, what is left is converted back in tranches with each amount public. Footer: public totals still bound hidden amounts and in a small book can pin them exactly.
2. Arrows: "funds it" bank to Dedicated Account: bank puts tokens in, amount public; clear. "one Convert" account to float: clear. The bracket from the float to the three suppliers with double-headed arrows: drawdowns out, repayments back; it is understandable only because the label says both, but the label "drawdowns and repayments: Sends" sits left, away from the arrows, and a reader may not tie it to them. The "At the end ... retired in tranches" arrow starts from a box not connected to the float above, so the reader must infer "what is left of the float" is the same float. The float box shows no arrow into that bottom row. "ENROLMENT" is a free-floating note with no arrow; it is not clear it relates to the supplier boxes to its right (it does, by position). The Dedicated Account box and "The float, confidential" are two boxes for what is conceptually one account; the subtitle "in the Dedicated Account" explains it.
3. Colour: dark outline on the float and dark bracket/arrows mean "the confidential part". The suppliers' boxes are not dark even though their contents are "hidden", so the dark colour tracks "where confidential transfers happen", not "what is hidden". The bank box says "reads every balance" in plain text with no emphasis, understating a crucial point compared with Figure 2.
4. Stronger than it should: the title says "It hides" and the diagram's heavy dark flow reads as a protected system; the caveat is only in the small italic footer. "limit, draws and what it owes: hidden" repeated on all three boxes reads strongly, and it is not said here that the bank (issuer) can read it, except in the bank box. "as it ran on Devnet" is good scoping.
5. Legibility: the ENROLMENT text is tight against the bottom of its box, nearly touching the border. Grey sublabels ("amount public", "each ConvertBack's amount public") are small and light. Otherwise fine.
6. Fixes: change "drawdowns and repayments: Sends" and place it adjacent to the arrows, or add "(both directions)". Link the bottom row, e.g. change "At the end / what is left of the float" to "At the end / what is left of the confidential float" and add a connector or "float" tag. Change "limit, draws and what it owes: hidden" to "limit, draws and what it owes: hidden from the public". Add "bank can read all of it" near the title or in the caveat footer, or move "reads every balance" into bold. Add bottom padding to the ENROLMENT box.
