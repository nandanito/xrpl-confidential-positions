## Findings

**High — The decision is bundled more strongly than the evidence supports.**  
The ADR supports “do not build natively on XLS-66.” It does not yet support “do not use an XLS-65 Vault object at all,” because rev 2 itself reopens the hybrid and says the answer depends on investor-side confidentiality. That means the decision currently contains a settled conclusion and an unsettled conclusion in one sentence.

Concretely: the ADR can be accepted for rejecting native XLS-66, but not for rejecting the hybrid until scope decides whether investor contribution, position, and redemption privacy matter. Recommended revision: split the decision into two decisions, e.g. “Native XLS-66 lending is rejected” and “Use of an XLS-65 Vault for funding remains open pending investor-side confidentiality scope.”

**High — The hybrid does not turn only on investor-side confidentiality.**  
That is the pivotal product question, but not the only material factor. Since ADR-0001 accepted a local signer with hostile same-host process in scope, the hybrid also turns on operational signer burden and transaction-risk surface.

F-23 matters here: a bad confidential proof returns `tecBAD_PROOF` while applying ciphertexts to the ledger, costing the amount. The hybrid may reduce how much custom pool accounting the application reimplements, but it still leaves confidential sends/repayments outside XLS-66 and therefore inside the local signer path. The ADR should ask: does the hybrid reduce, increase, or leave unchanged the number and complexity of confidential operations the local signer must construct? That is not just implementation detail after F-23; it is user-loss risk.

Add a comparison row: **Local-signer / failed-proof exposure**: §9 and hybrid both rely on confidential sends for supplier flows; hybrid may reduce app-layer funding accounting but does not remove proof-risk from drawdowns and repayments.

**Medium — The hybrid table is honest but incomplete.**  
The rows it includes are fair. It correctly withdraws the false “extra public hop” argument and identifies investor contribution, position, and redemption as the real disclosure delta.

Missing rows that could change weighing:

| Value / concern | §9 application-logic | Hybrid with XLS-65 vault |
|---|---|---|
| Pool/share accounting correctness | custom app logic | protocol-native vault/share accounting |
| Withdrawal policy enforcement | custom app logic | protocol-native vault withdrawal path |
| Investor KYC/domain policy surface | one application model | vault pseudo-account/domain exemption enters compliance model |
| Local-signer proof-risk exposure | confidential supplier flows | confidential supplier flows, plus any bridge/conversion choreography |
| Protocol upgrade dependency | mostly XLS-96 plus domain amendments | XLS-96 plus XLS-65/66-adjacent behavior and vault semantics |

The first two rows favor the hybrid. The next two may favor §9. The ADR mentions these in prose but should put them in the table because the table is doing the weighing.

**Medium — Corrected blocker 3 is directionally right, but stated too narrowly.**  
Rev 2 is right to withdraw the “amortisation is impossible under additive homomorphism” claim. Public-scalar multiplication is not the mathematical blocker.

But the replacement should not imply that only “rounding, fixed-point truncation” need a new proof relation. A confidential XLS-66 would also need protocol changes for confidential loan fields, validator-enforced balance updates, equality/linkage between confidential payment movement and loan state, range/non-negativity constraints, fees, interest accrual, overpayment handling, and public/private boundary rules. Rounding/truncation are examples of non-linear constraints, not the whole obstacle.

Recommended sentence change:

Current:
> closing it would require a new proof relation for the non-linear part (rounding, fixed-point truncation), a specification, and validator-side changes.

Revise to:
> closing it would require confidential loan-state fields, new transaction semantics, and proof relations for the parts validators currently check in plaintext, including linkage to payments, range/non-negativity constraints, and non-linear fixed-point effects such as rounding and truncation.

**Low — GAP-8 status is stale and materially misleading.**  
The ADR says two of fifteen transactor paths were executed, but the prompt says the full fifteen-transactor lifecycle closed on 2026-08-17. Since this is in the acceptance list, leaving it stale makes the ADR look less supported than it is.

Revise item 3 to say GAP-8 closed on 2026-08-17 with fifteen transactors executed, and update any grades tied to E-40/F-10 accordingly.

## Explicit Answers

1. **Does the decision still follow?**  
Partly. Rejecting native XLS-66 still follows from blockers 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Rejecting any XLS-65 Vault use does not yet follow, because the hybrid remains open. The ADR currently has a correct native-XLS-66 conclusion but overstates the settled scope of the final architecture.

2. **Is corrected blocker 3 right this time?**  
Mostly yes. Rev 2 fixes the mathematical error. The remaining claim should be broadened: the blocker is not just non-linear rounding/truncation; it is that XLS-66’s validator checks, loan fields, and payment/state linkage are plaintext today and would need a confidential protocol design.

3. **Is the hybrid comparison table complete and honest?**  
Honest, not complete. It fairly states the confidentiality delta, but omits rows for native share/accounting benefits, withdrawal enforcement, compliance/domain complexity, and local-signer failed-proof exposure.

4. **What does the ADR claim that its evidence does not support?**  
It claims the facility “does not use an XLS-65 Vault object” before the hybrid is resolved. It also claims the hybrid turns on one question, when local-signer/proof-risk and protocol accounting correctness also turn on the choice.

5. **Should this ADR be accepted, revised again, or rejected?**  
**Revised again.** Do not accept while the hybrid remains open and GAP-8 text is stale. The main required revision is to split the decision: native XLS-66 rejected; hybrid/XLS-65 Vault funding side unresolved pending investor-side confidentiality scope and the added operational-risk rows.
