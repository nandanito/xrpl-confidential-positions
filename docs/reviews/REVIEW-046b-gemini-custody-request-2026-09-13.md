## THE OPTIONS, one line each

- **Option 1 (Accept with disclosure):** Leaves £50m movable and readable under a single unrotatable hot key, providing zero technical safeguards and maximizing the bank's balance-sheet and liability exposure.
- **Option 2a (SignerList + `lsfDisableMaster` + derived key):** Mitigates unilateral asset theft via multisig quorum while retaining the master seed as an unrotatable single point of failure for ElGamal decryption and ZKP generation.
- **Option 2b (SignerList + `lsfDisableMaster` + standalone key):** Separates quorum spending authority from an independent ElGamal decryption secret, eliminating master-seed dual exposure but requiring long-term custody of an unrotatable ElGamal secret.
- **Option 3 (No standing float / Convert per drawdown):** Eliminates standing float custody risk at the cost of destroying facility confidentiality by publishing every drawdown amount and schedule on-ledger in plaintext.
- **Option 4 (XLS-65 Vault pseudo-account):** Achieves keyless non-custodial pooling but forfeits confidential balances entirely, as pseudo-accounts cannot hold confidential MPTs and all loan terms become public.
- **Option 5 (Partition float across Dedicated Accounts):** Compartmentalizes the blast radius of a key leak across tranches/epochs, but multiplies account overhead, increases plaintext conversion events, and leaks counterparty groupings via the public transaction graph.

## THE RECOMMENDATION'S REASONING

The recommendation's reasoning is **fundamentally unsound and incomplete on its own terms**:

1. **False Equivalence of Distinct Risk Classes:** It argues that because Option 2 cannot solve Power 2 (decryption), the bank should not implement Option 2 to solve Power 1 (movement). For a bank custody officer, the outright loss/theft of £50,000,000 of principal (Power 1) is a catastrophic prudential failure; an information disclosure of cash-flow timing (Power 2) is a data privacy breach. Conflating them to dismiss multisig movement controls is a serious risk fallacy.
2. **Prioritizing Implementation Effort over Custodial Risk:** It rejects Option 2 because modifying the signer path is "the most expensive place in this system to change anything." Recommending that a bank hold £50m under a single unrotatable hot seed to avoid client-side engineering work is commercially and fiduciarily unacceptable.
3. **Omission of Critical Protocol Powers:** The reasoning evaluates the custody boundary as if the Dedicated Account seed is the sole locus of risk, entirely omitting Issuer Clawback, Issuer Token Locks, and Delegate Account authorities.
4. **Failure to Address Operational Destruction:** It ignores the protocol reality that losing the single key under Option 1 locks the float permanently (Spec §A.6), and that the only protocol recourse—Clawback—permanently burns the bank's capital rather than recovering it (Spec §12.1).
