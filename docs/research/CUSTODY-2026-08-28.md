## Finding 1 — the ruled key derivation is incompatible with custodial key management

**The chain, each link in this repository:**

1. A confidential operation requires the **ElGamal secret** — to decrypt the balance being spent and to produce the proof — in addition to the XRPL account key that signs.
2. Under the ruled key generation (`docs/reviews/REVIEW-012-RULING-keygen-2026-08-18.md`, implemented in `signer/src/keygen.rs`), the ElGamal secret is **derived from the account's master family seed**.
3. A custody platform's entire purpose is **never to release seed material**. Releasing it would defeat the custody.

**Therefore a custodied account cannot derive its own viewing key.** It cannot read its own confidential balance, which is not a limitation of any integration — it follows from step 2 alone.

⚠ **The escape hatch exists and it moves the problem rather than solving it.** `KeySource::Standalone` (`signer/src/onboarding.rs:279`) is an independent ElGamal secret with its own backup, and it is what every profile written before 2026-08-20 silently was. But then **something outside the custody boundary holds a permanent decryption credential for every balance the account has ever held** — and *who holds it* is `CLAIMS.md` **E-117**, which is open and has had no adversarial review.

⚠ **E-117's axis is the honest framing, and it is sharper than a custody question:** *confidential positions, or non-custodial pooling — one or the other, today.* This project picks confidentiality because that is its premise. A design that needs *"no single party can move the float"* weighs it the other way, and ~~the comparison table in `ADR-0002` **has no row for custody at all**~~ **(struck 2026-09-13, `CLAIMS.md` E-295: the row has existed since 2026-08-20, and the same clause was struck at `:86` one round earlier without reaching this copy)**.

**What would change this:** a derivation whose input is not the master seed, or a custody platform that performs the derivation inside its own boundary and never emits either secret. **The second is not obviously impossible** — it is a key-derivation function over a seed the platform already holds — and it is the question worth putting to a custody vendor. ⚠ **It would require the derivation to be a published standard, which is issue #10, open, and no code discharges it.**

## Finding 2 — a policy engine cannot enforce an amount limit on a confidential amount

⚠⚠ **This is the capital-markets crux, and it is structural rather than an integration detail.**

Custodial approval workflows are written over transaction *values*: *"require two approvers above $1M"*, *"block transfers to unlisted counterparties"*, *"cap daily outflow"*. A `ConfidentialMPTSend` carries **a proof, not a value**. The amount is exactly what has been hidden, and it is hidden from *everyone* holding only public data — which includes the policy engine.

**So the first rule is unevaluable.** Not hard to implement: unevaluable, because the input does not exist in the data the engine sees.

**What survives, and it is worth being precise about:**

| policy shape | on a confidential send | why |
|---|---|---|
| *"require N approvers above amount X"* | ⚠ **unevaluable** | the amount is a proof |
| *"cap total daily outflow"* | ⚠ **unevaluable** | sums of hidden amounts are hidden |
| *"only these destinations"* | **evaluable** | the destination is public (P-4, P-11) |
| *"only during these hours"* | **evaluable** | timing is public (P-11) |
| *"only this issuance"* | **evaluable** | the issuance is public |
| *"require N approvers, unconditionally"* | **evaluable** | no amount is read |
| *"block if the account is frozen"* | **evaluable** | ledger state is public |

⚠ **The pattern: every policy over an IDENTITY, a TIME or a COUNT survives; every policy over a VALUE does not.** That is P-4 and P-11 restated as a custody constraint — this system hides amounts and hides nothing else — and it is the single most useful sentence in this document for a capital-markets reader.

**Three ways out, and none is free:**

1. **Give the policy engine the ElGamal secret.** Then it can decrypt and evaluate — and it is now a party that can read every balance, which is Finding 1's problem with a second holder added.
2. **Have the holder attest the amount alongside the proof.** The engine then enforces a policy over a *claim*, not over the transaction. ⚠ **An attestation the engine cannot verify against the proof is a policy over an honest holder's honesty**, which is not a control.
3. **Prove the bound in zero knowledge.** *"This transfer is under $1M"* is a range proof — and **XLS-0096 already carries range proofs**, so the machinery is present rather than hypothetical. Verified at source: a `ConfidentialMPTSend`'s 946-byte proof is 192 B sigma **plus a 754 B aggregated Bulletproof over two 64-bit values**, and a `ConfidentialMPTConvertBack`'s 816 B is 128 B sigma plus a **688 B single Bulletproof**; TASK ZERO records that *"the aggregated Bulletproof proves the amount lies in [0, 2⁶⁴)"* (`docs/task-zero/TASK-ZERO-2026-08-13.md`, Grade A, sizes confirmed by execution against `mpt-crypto`).

   ⚠ **What is present is a range proof over a FIXED bound — [0, 2⁶⁴), the field's own validity condition — not over a POLICY bound chosen by an approver.** Those are different proofs: one is a protocol invariant every transaction already carries, the other is a per-policy statement nobody has specified, generated by the holder, verified by the engine, and bound to the same amount the transaction moves. ⚠ **This project has neither built nor costed the second, and the gap between them is the whole question.** Recorded as a direction, not a recommendation.

## Finding 3 — tranche sizes are public even when the balance was not

A custody client would reasonably assume that a confidentially-held position is retired confidentially. It is not.

`MPTAmount` is a **plaintext field on every individual `ConfidentialMPTConvertBack`**, so retiring a position in tranches publishes **every tranche size** and its timing. `CLAIMS.md` **F-24**, found in this project's own facility walkthrough after that document had already passed `claims-check`, and read off two validated Devnet transactions at 95 and 191.

⚠ **For a custody client this is a reporting-surface question, not a cryptography question**, and it is the kind that surfaces after a position is opened rather than before.

## Finding 4 — the residual custody risk this project already carries

`CLAIMS.md` **E-117** again, in its step-7 form: the facility's Dedicated Account is **an ordinary XRPL account with an ordinary key**, so whoever holds that key can move the entire float. **Incomplete, `CLAIMS.md` E-290:** the issuer can also erase and freeze the float without that key, and the key that decrypts is also required to spend. The XLS-65 `Vault` that was rejected has a pseudo-account with **no key**, and nothing can move its assets outside the protocol's rules.

⚠ **The rejection was correct on its own grounds** — `Vault` share tokens cannot be confidential, which forces native share accounting XOR confidential investor positions — ~~**but the comparison table counts only disclosure and accounting.** It has no custody row.~~ **Struck 2026-09-13 (`CLAIMS.md` E-292): the table has had a custody row since `c34dcec` on 2026-08-20, and E-256 struck the same clause in E-117 on 2026-09-09 without reaching this copy.** This document does not reopen that decision; it records that the cost is unpriced and that a custody conversation will surface it immediately.

## The demo mode, and what it is

`web/app` renders a **custody-shaped key source**: a standalone ElGamal key plus an external-signer stub standing in for a custody platform, showing which flows survive and which break.

⚠⚠ **It is a SIMULATION and every surface says so.** There is no custody integration, no MPC and no HSM. `CLAIMS.md` **P-5** forbids claiming more than is delivered, and *"custody-compatible"* on a screen with a stub behind it is exactly that kind of claim. **`web/api/disclosure.test.ts` fails if any surface stops saying it**, and that test is verified by breaking it.

- **That Harmonize cannot do any of this.** It makes no claim about Harmonize at all; see the opening section.

## Open, and worth putting to a custody vendor

1. Can the platform derive a secondary key from a seed **inside its boundary** and emit neither? (Finding 1's only clean exit, and it needs issue #10 closed.)
2. Can the policy engine consume a **range proof over a policy bound** rather than a value? ⚠ Note the protocol's existing Bulletproofs prove a **fixed** range, not a chosen one — the two are not the same artifact. (Finding 2's third way out.)
3. How does the platform's reporting surface handle a position whose size it cannot read? (Finding 3.)
