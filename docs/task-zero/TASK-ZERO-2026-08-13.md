## 6. Cost

**Size. ~~Grade A on the total, B on the components~~ Grade A throughout, MEASURED ON THE WIRE 2026-08-17** (spec §14.1, `Nciphers = 4`): 264 B ciphertexts + 66 B Pedersen commitments + 946 B ZKProof = **1276 B with auditor, 1210 B without** — both figures confirmed by accepted Devnet transactions at n=4 and n=3 respectively, not by arithmetic. Signed blob 1492 B / 1423 B. **The competing 1177 B in `cmpt-compact-sigma.pdf` Table 2 is refuted for the wire**: `C₁` is shared, and protocol-enforced as shared, but transmitted once per participant. See CLAIMS.md E-4 and F-13. `kTxMaxSizeBytes = megabytes(1)` — **Grade A, executed**. Size is not a binding constraint.

*Open:* `kOversizeMetaDataCap = 5200`. Confidential state updates write ciphertexts into metadata for both parties plus mirrors. Whether that approaches the cap has not been checked. Check it before asserting anything about ledger impact.

**Fee. Grade A.** `Protocol.h:492` — `kConfidentialFeeMultiplier = 9`, passed by `ConfidentialMPTSend::calculateBaseFee`. RippleX docs independently give **100 drops** before load scaling for a single-signed confidential transaction, against 10 for a standard one; multi-signed is `10 × (10 + signatures)`. *The exact arithmetic relating the constant to the result was not verified — `Transactor::calculateBaseFee` was not read. Two sources agree on 100 drops; the mechanism is stated as inference.*

Fee escalation multiplies that base as it does any transaction. **The 10× fee floor, not byte count, is what a high-throughput confidential application budgets for.**

**Reserve. Grade A.** An `MPToken` is an owner-reserve object, and spec §7.4 adds a **deletion blocker**: once confidential fields are initialised the `MPToken` can never be deleted, *even at zero balance*. The reserve is locked for the life of the account. A holder who opts in once can never reclaim it. `MPTokenIssuance` can only be deleted when `sfConfidentialOutstandingAmount` is 0.

**Timing. ~~UNUSABLE.~~ REPLACED 2026-08-16.** Spec §14.2's ~44.8 ms / ~22.6 ms on a "laptop-class CPU" remain unusable under P-8 and are still never to be quoted. Measured replacements, full hardware and flags in `benchmarks-2026-08-16.md`: on an Apple M3 Pro under clang 21 `-O3`, aggregated Bulletproof (m=2, the Send case) proving **median 34.583 ms** and verification **median 16.100 ms** over 50 runs; the 192 B compact sigma adds **0.214 ms** proving and **0.328 ms** verification. **The sigma proofs are free and the Bulletproof is the entire cost** — the sigma component is 0.6% of proving and 2.0% of verification.

**Decryption is the cost nobody budgeted for.** It is not in §14.2 at all, and at the default window it is two orders of magnitude more expensive than proving: ~2.5 s versus ~35 ms. See §6 of the benchmarks and CLAIMS.md F-7.

## 7. Compliance gating — mechanism Grade A, availability folded into GAP-1

`ConfidentialMPTSend` has no `DomainID` field. Its preclaim calls `requireAuth` on **both sender and destination**, then `credentials::valid`, then `checkDepositPreauth` — explicitly *before* the expensive proof verification — and only then verifies proofs.

The domain gate lives inside `requireAuth` (`MPTokenHelpers.cpp`): it reads `sfDomainID` from the `MPTokenIssuance`, asserts `lsfMPTRequireAuth`, and calls `credentials::validDomain`. **So Permissioned Domain membership does gate confidential transfers, at the issuance level, enforced on both parties.**

**The dependency nobody would predict. Grade A.** `MPTokenIssuanceCreate.cpp:31–36`:

```cpp
if (ctx.tx.isFieldPresent(sfDomainID) &&
    !(ctx.rules.enabled(featurePermissionedDomains) &&
      ctx.rules.enabled(featureSingleAssetVault)))
```

Writing `sfDomainID` requires **`PermissionedDomains` *and* `SingleAssetVault` (XLS-65)**. Both are `Supported::Yes`, so this is not blocked on unshipped code — it is blocked on what the target network has enabled.

**W-CONF therefore needs three amendments live on the Confidential Devnet simultaneously: `ConfidentialTransfer`, `PermissionedDomains` and `SingleAssetVault`.** A devnet stood up specifically for XLS-96 may carry the first without the others. This is the most likely way the compliance third of the project dies, and `tools/check-amendments.ts` already checks all three.

**N-1. Grade A.** `MPTokenIssuanceSet.cpp:369–376` — setting `sfDomainID` to zero calls `makeFieldAbsent`. **The issuer can unilaterally remove the domain gate at any time, with no holder consent.** Belongs in the trust model alongside clawback.

## 8. Findings — the documentation contradicts the specification in four places, and is wrong in all four

Revised after the verification round. This is now a documentation-accuracy finding, not a claim about protocol weakness — the earlier framing risked presenting a documented design choice as a discovered flaw.

**Each of the four was checked against the rippled 3.3.0 transactors. The implementation agrees with the specification every time.**

**F-1 — The canonical encrypted zero is publicly detectable. Grade A, verified.**
Docs: the post-merge inbox value *"is indistinguishable from other ciphertexts to observers without the private key."* Spec §9.4: validators can compare against the known `EncZero` and, if they match, *"the balance is publicly known to be exactly 0."*

`encryptCanonicalZeroAmount(pubKey, account, mptIssuanceID)` is called in all three places:
- `ConfidentialMPTConvert.cpp:305–313` — first-time opt-in initialises the spending balance
- `ConfidentialMPTMergeInbox.cpp:111–114` — inbox reset after merge
- `ConfidentialMPTClawback.cpp:138–166` — spending, inbox, issuer mirror and auditor mirror

All three inputs are public. Any observer can recompute and byte-compare. **A freshly-opted-in holder's spending balance, and any holder's inbox after a merge, are publicly identifiable as exactly zero.** The documentation is wrong. Balances that reach zero by homomorphic subtraction remain opaque — that is what spec §10.7.2 describes, and the documentation generalises the wrong case.

**F-2 — "Issuer-mediated auditing" is not scoped disclosure. Grade A on the mechanism, B on the consequence.**
Spec §13.2.2 describes *"a separate set of view keys."* **No such field exists** — `MPTokenIssuance` carries exactly `sfIssuerEncryptionKey` and `sfAuditorEncryptionKey`. The docs describe the real mechanism: the issuer shares its own ElGamal private key, granting *"the same decryption capabilities as the issuer."*

Stated in two layers, per Gemini's correction:
- **Protocol capability:** issuer keys are permanent and cannot be rotated.
- **Operational consequence:** an issuer who chooses Mechanism 2 grants disclosure that can never be withdrawn, because the key can never be rotated.

The second follows from the first only once the choice is made. Prefer the on-chain auditor key.

**F-3 — Enabling confidentiality is one-way. Grade A, verified.**
Docs: *"can be toggled on and off as long as no confidential balances exist."* Spec §6.3.3: one-way, no disable flag. `MPTokenIssuanceSet.cpp` handles `tfMPTSetCanHoldConfidentialBalance` and `tifMPTCanHoldConfidentialBalance` only; there is no clear flag anywhere in its flag handling. The documentation is wrong. *(Method: absence across the transactor plus the spec's explicit statement. Weaker than a positive read — re-derive from the full flag enumeration if it matters.)*

**F-4 — Clawback increments the version counter. Grade A, verified.**
Docs: *"the version counter is reset to 0."* `ConfidentialMPTClawback.cpp:150` calls `incrementConfidentialVersion`. The documentation is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that breaks integrations: a wallet predicting a post-clawback version of 0 binds proofs to the wrong value and fails with `tecBAD_PROOF`.

**Two specification errata, separate from the above:**

**F-5 — Grade A, executed.** Spec §11.1 instructs the issuer to set a `RevealedAmount` field. `grep -i revealed sfields.macro` returns nothing. The implemented field is `sfMPTAmount`, which spec §11.2's own table names correctly. The prose is stale.

**F-6 — Grade A, verified.** Spec §11 and §11.4 say clawed-back funds are burned; the closing sentence of §11.2 says they move to the issuer's public reserve. `ConfidentialMPTClawback.cpp:170–179` decrements both `sfConfidentialOutstandingAmount` and `sfOutstandingAmount` with no credit to any account. Funds burn. The §11.2 sentence is the error.

**Publication order.** File all four documentation errors and both errata upstream first. Publish second. F-4 and F-1 have operational consequences for integrators, which is what makes them worth reporting rather than merely noting.

## 9. Domain recommendation — revised

The previous revision claimed the restructured facility "shows nothing about which supplier drew how much." **All three review passes independently found that false, and they were right.** Sender and receiver are public on every transfer, so an observer sees which supplier drew, when, and how often. Only the quantity is hidden. This section is rewritten.

### What the restructure actually buys

Value must enter confidentiality **once, in aggregate**, and every per-participant event must be a `ConfidentialMPTSend`:

1. The funding bank issues the MPT and creates a Dedicated Account — the issuer account is forbidden from holding confidential balances of its own issuance.
2. The bank funds the vault publicly with the whole facility float; the vault converts **once**. Public: total facility size — which investors need.

   > **Terminology hazard, flagged 2026-08-16 (ADR-0002).** "The vault" here means the **Dedicated Account** of step 1 — an ordinary XRPL account — **not** an XLS-65 `Vault` ledger object. That distinction was harmless when written and is now load-bearing: an XLS-65 `Vault` pseudo-account **cannot** convert, so reading the word as the ledger object makes this step impossible. Prefer "Dedicated Account" throughout from here on.
3. Every drawdown is a `ConfidentialMPTSend` from vault to supplier. Amount hidden.
4. Every repayment is a `ConfidentialMPTSend` back. Amount hidden.
5. `ConvertBack` only when the facility is retired or resized. Public: aggregate change.

**Precisely:** this eliminates plaintext disclosure through `Convert` events. It does not eliminate metadata leakage. Remaining public: the counterparty set, the fact and timing of every drawdown and repayment, drawdown frequency per supplier, aggregate facility size, and `ConfidentialOutstandingAmount`.

### Does the domain survive the residual leak?

The original justification was that drawdown level and financing terms reveal a supplier's cash position. Assessed against what actually leaks:

- **Drawdown level** — hidden. The justification's core holds.
- **Financing terms** — never on-ledger at all. `TransferFee` must be 0 for confidential issuances, so fees and interest are computed off-ledger regardless.
- **Cash position inferred from drawdown frequency** — **leaks.** A supplier moving from monthly to weekly draws is legible as stress without any amount being known.

**Recommendation: the domain survives, conditional on the application generating scheduled cover traffic.** *(New argument. **Still Proposed and still unreviewed as an argument.** Revised 2026-08-17 — see the corrections immediately below, which both REVIEW-004 legs forced.)*

> **CORRECTION, 2026-08-17.** This paragraph previously asserted that a zero-amount send "is indistinguishable from a non-zero send" as settled fact, and rested the whole recommendation on it. **That overstated the evidence and was the highest-severity finding of REVIEW-004** (Codex leg finding 1; Gemini HIGH-01). What is now established, and what is not:
>
> - **Validity is settled — Grade A, executed.** A zero-amount `ConfidentialMPTSend` is accepted and validated on Devnet. That is CLAIMS.md E-22(a) and it is the limb that would have killed this recommendation outright.
> - **Indistinguishability is NOT settled and cannot be settled by this method.** It survived three falsification checks under a controlled rerun — randomisation across repeats, non-match against recomputed canonical `EncZero`, and a like-for-like comparison against non-zero sends at an identical sender balance with the fee threshold measured on both arms. **Surviving three attempts to break it is not a proof.** That is E-22(b), graded C.
> - **The count itself was wrong.** An earlier write-up claimed "four checks"; the first of the four was validity, which is limb (a), so the count used one claim as evidence for a different one.
> - **What this recommendation actually needs has not been built:** an adversary model over transaction *sequences*. Every check to date compares one transaction against another. Cover traffic is a claim about traffic patterns over time — timing, counterparty graph, `sfDestinationTag`, and correlation against the issuer's plaintext `sfConfidentialOutstandingAmount`. None of that is tested.
>
> **Treat cover traffic as an unverified proposal, not a mitigation, until that model exists.** See CLAIMS.md B-10 and `REVIEW-004c-adjudication-2026-08-17.md` §2.

The protocol supports it natively as far as *validity* goes: a `ConfidentialMPTSend` of zero is a valid transaction — the aggregated Bulletproof proves the amount lies in [0, 2⁶⁴), and zero is in range — and its ciphertexts are freshly randomised rather than canonical. The canonical `EncZero` of F-1 is used only for initialisation and resets, never for send amounts.

So the *shape* of the mitigation is: the vault sends to **every** enrolled supplier on a fixed cadence, whether or not value moves. If indistinguishability holds, drawdown frequency becomes a constant and stops carrying signal. Cost is one transaction per supplier per period at **10× the network base fee** — trivial. *(Corrected 2026-08-17: this previously said "100 drops", which is the ratio against a base fee of 10. The multiplier is the durable fact and was measured on 2026-08-17; the absolute number is network-dependent — CLAIMS.md E-5.)* What still leaks is the counterparty set: who is enrolled on the facility. That is generally not secret, since a buyer's supplier list is usually discoverable, and it should be disclosed as accepted rather than defended.

If cover traffic is judged too artificial for a reference application, the honest alternative is to keep the domain and state plainly that timing is public — but then the commercial justification is weaker than the prompt claims, and that must be said in the README rather than glossed.

### The alternative that may make this moot

> **ANSWERED 2026-08-16 — it does not make it moot. See `XLS-66-INVESTIGATION-2026-08-16.md`.**
> XLS-66 models a fixed-term amortising loan with no revolving drawdowns, and **no asset inside the lending protocol can carry a confidential balance** — the vault share MPT cannot be created with the flag and cannot acquire it later, and vault and broker pseudo-accounts can neither convert into nor receive a confidential balance because they cannot sign. Separately, every loan and vault value is a plaintext ledger field. **Recommendation: build as application logic. The domain does not move.** Tested on Devnet, not only read.
> Two consequences for the rest of this section: §9's facility design stands unchanged, and the second carry-forward item below is **dissolved rather than answered** — a pseudo-account cannot hold a confidential balance, so nobody tracks one. The first carry-forward item is unchanged and still reviewed by nobody.

The reading that raised the question, retained for context:

`LendingProtocol` (XLS-66) is **enabled on standard Devnet as of 2026-08-16** — not merely `Supported::Yes` — alongside all three amendments W-CONF needs (E-26). It has native `Vault`, `LoanBroker` and `Loan` ledger objects. Before committing to application-layer facility logic, establish whether XLS-66 already models the facility natively and whether its share tokens can be confidential MPTs. If they can, the project becomes "confidential positions in a native lending vault" — sharper, and resting on two amendments instead of one. If they cannot, that incompatibility is itself a finding. **This is the highest-value hour available and it should precede the naming sweep**, since it may change the domain and therefore the name.

#### Two things above must not be inherited silently if the domain moves

*Added 2026-08-16. Both are load-bearing parts of §9 that are weaker than they look, and a change of domain is exactly the moment they get carried across without re-examination.*

1. **The cover-traffic recommendation is `Proposed` and reviewed by nobody.** It is the sole reason §9 concludes the domain survives its residual metadata leak. It also depends on **E-22, which is Grade C — inferred, not tested.** If the facility moves into a native XLS-66 vault, the counterparty and timing leaks change shape entirely: drawdowns may become `Loan` object state transitions rather than `ConfidentialMPTSend`s, and a cover-traffic scheme designed for the latter may be irrelevant, insufficient, or impossible. **Re-derive the leak analysis for the new mechanism. Do not port the conclusion.**

2. **R-5 was un-retracted on 2026-08-16** (see CLAIMS.md). The single-conversion facility design — the vault converting the whole float in one `ConfidentialMPTConvert` — **is live again**, because the 1,000,000 decryption bound turned out to be a caller-supplied search window rather than a ceiling (F-7). What replaced the float cap is a **balance-tracking requirement**: whoever holds the confidential balance must maintain its expected value off-ledger to decrypt cheaply, and resynchronisation costs ~2.49 µs per unit of uncertainty with no sublinear fallback shipped (F-8; true at mpt-crypto `1.0.4`, and upstream added one in `1.0.5`, E-297). **If a native vault pseudo-account holds the confidential balance, establish who performs that tracking and whether a pseudo-account can.** That question has no answer yet and it is not obviously favourable.

**Neither of these blocks the XLS-66 investigation. Both block accepting its recommendation.**

### Constraints to design around

- **`TransferFee` must be 0.** Fees and interest settle off-ledger or as separate confidential sends.
- **Clawback burns the holder's entire balance** and cannot model partial recovery. It is a sanctions instrument, not a repayment mechanism.

### Why not the confidential fund share register

Same plaintext-conversion problem at subscription and redemption, no secondary market (XLS-96 forbids DEX integration; XLS-82 is `Supported::No`), and it is the example Ripple's own material uses.
