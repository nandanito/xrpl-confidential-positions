## The thing the row does not say: there are two powers, and they are not independent

⚠ **This heading said *"only one of them is splittable"* until `REVIEW-046`.** The key that decrypts is also a required input to every confidential spend (H2), so neither power can be fully separated from the other. And neither is held by the Dedicated Account alone — see *Who else holds power over the float*, below.

E-117 describes a single exposure — *"whoever holds that key can move the entire float"*. The Dedicated Account's seed confers **two** distinct powers, and the decision looks different once they are separated.

**Power 1 — movement.** Whoever holds the seed can `drawdown` the whole confidential float to any enrolled address (`facility.ts:441`) or `retire` it back to public MPT and pay it away (`facility.ts:516-537`).

**Power 2 — decryption, of the whole book.** A `ConfidentialMPTSend` carries the same amount encrypted once per recipient as separate signed fields — sender, destination and issuer always (`signer/src/xrpl.rs:1184`, `:1186`, `:1182`), **plus an auditor ciphertext only when the issuance carries an auditor key** (`:1191`) — all sharing one `C₁ = r·G` because they encrypt the same value under shared randomness (`docs/task-zero/GAP-3-compact-sigma-2026-08-16.md:68`), with the sigma proof enforcing consistency across whichever set is present. ⚠ **This facility's issuance sets no auditor key**, so its sends carry **three**, not the four the spec's `n=4` analysis assumes: `facility/run-facility.ts:349-354` sets `IssuerEncryptionKey` alone, and the auditor arm is appended conditionally at `signer/src/txn.rs:795-799`. **The custody conclusion does not depend on the count** — the Dedicated Account holds one of the arms on every transfer either way. The signer decrypts its own arm from the transaction itself (`signer/src/txn.rs:39`), and `txn.rs:785-797` is where whichever arms are present are read as one ciphertext set — `:795-799` appends the auditor arm only `if let Some(..)`, so for this facility that loop reads **three**. Every drawdown and every repayment has the Dedicated Account as one endpoint, by construction — `facility.ts:441` and `:446` route both through `cfg.dedicated`. So its key decrypts the amount of **every per-supplier movement in the facility**, not just the float total. This is the per-supplier confidentiality the whole design exists to provide. ⚠ **It is not the only whole-book reader** (`REVIEW-046` H3): the issuer's ElGamal secret reads every amount and every balance in the issuance, and the facility's own book holds every confidential amount in plaintext.

⚠ **Power 2 cannot be rotated for a given account, at any level.** The Dedicated Account's ElGamal key is derived from its master seed (`facility.ts`, `deriveElGamalKey(p.seed)`), and XLS-96 supports neither rotation nor recovery (P-10) — a registered `HolderEncryptionKey` is fixed for the life of that `MPToken` (E-9). A seed that leaks once reads that account's whole book for ever, past movements included, and **nothing can un-read them**. ⚠ **What it does not read is another account**: replacing the Dedicated Account bounds a leak to the movements made before the replacement, which is the only sense in which anything rotates here. That is Option 5.

## Who else holds power over the float — added 2026-09-13 by `REVIEW-046`

All three legs of that round found this independently, the specification-only leg included. Each point is verified at source in the adjudication.

- **The decrypting key is spending authority** (H2). The Send proof takes *"the sender's 32-byte private key"* and proves *"balance ownership"* (`mpt_utility.h:233-269`); the ConvertBack proof's witness includes it (`:272-291`); the signer passes it into every Send proof (`signer/src/prover.rs:724-733`). XLS-0096 §5.5 says the holder key *"carries exclusive spending authority over the account's confidential balance"*. **Confidential movement needs an XRPL signature AND a proof from the ElGamal secret.** Whoever holds that secret can veto every drawdown and retirement, must have it on hand for each one, and if it is lost the balance is *"effectively locked and irrecoverable"* (§A.6).
- **The issuer can erase the float** (H1). This facility's issuance sets `tfMPTCanClawback` (`facility/run-facility.ts:323`). One `ConfidentialMPTClawback` from the bank, proved with the issuer's ElGamal secret, sets the Dedicated Account's confidential balances to encrypted zero and burns the amount (rippled `3.3.0` `ConfidentialMPTClawback.cpp:72-179`). It needs neither the Dedicated Account's key nor any quorum on it.
- **The issuer can immobilise the float** (H1). The issuance was created without `ImmutableFlags`, so under `DynamicMPT` the bank can set `CanLock` and then lock (`MPTokenIssuanceSet.cpp:142-197`), after which Send and ConvertBack refuse (`ConfidentialMPTSend.cpp:246-249`, `ConfidentialMPTConvertBack.cpp:198`). ⚠ **A second mechanism, added by `REVIEW-047` F3:** the bank can require authorisation, which holder-created tokens never had (`MPTokenHelpers.cpp:223-231`); `requireAuth` then refuses Send, ConvertBack and MergeInbox (`MPTokenHelpers.cpp:420-422`). The Dedicated Account's token on Devnet has `Flags: 0`. **Both read at source, not executed.**
- **The issuer's ElGamal secret reads the whole book** (H3), every `IssuerEncryptedAmount` and every `IssuerEncryptedBalance` (P-9), and cannot be rotated (F-2, P-10). In this facility it is a standalone keypair (`run-facility.ts:346`).
- **So does anyone who can read the facility's book** (H3). `facility/src/book.ts:47-49` says it holds every confidential movement's amount and every expected balance *"in plaintext, on disk, unencrypted"*. ⚠ **Two corrections, `REVIEW-047` F9 and F10:** this said *"every amount"*, but public payments are not in the book; and it said the file *"is mode `0644`"*, which was true until B-29 wrote it `0600`.
- **A delegate can sign movements in the account's name** (H4). Send, ConvertBack and MergeInbox are delegable (`transactions.macro:1106-1136`), a `Delegate` object authorises it (`Transactor.cpp:381-408`), and `PermissionDelegationV1_1` is enabled on Devnet. Nothing in `signer/` or `facility/` reads a `Delegate` object. ⚠ **The issuer side too, added by `REVIEW-047` F4:** `MPTokenIssuanceSet` and `ConfidentialMPTClawback` are delegable (`transactions.macro:727`, `:1157`), so an issuer delegate can lock or require authorisation without the bank's seed, and can supply the signature half of a clawback, whose proof still needs the issuer's ElGamal secret.
- **Whoever controls the ledger source** can make the signer encrypt an amount to a substituted key (F-23, executed; F-28; B-15). No custody option changes that, and it is cross-referenced here rather than re-priced (L2).
- **Anything running as this user that can read `~/.wconf/facility-state.json`** acquires every seed and the issuer's ElGamal secret (`REVIEW-047` F5). `0600` keeps other users out and does not stop this one.
- **Not powers over the float, recorded so their absence is not read as an omission** (`REVIEW-047` F1). Each supplier's key reads and moves only that supplier's own balance and amounts (`signer/src/prover.rs:675-680`). Any receiving account, the Dedicated Account included, can refuse confidential transfers into itself with DepositAuth (`ConfidentialMPTSend.cpp:263-268`).

### Why the three obvious XRPL fixes do not work as stated

**A signer list alone removes nothing, and a regular key alone removes nothing.** This is not inference, and it is verified at the primary source rather than inherited from our own comment about it. rippled 3.3.0 `src/libxrpl/tx/Transactor.cpp`, fetched 2026-09-11: `checkSign` dispatches to `checkMultiSign` **only when the transaction itself carries `sfSigners`** (`:976-979`), so a singly-signed transaction from an account that has a signer list still reaches `checkSingleSign`, where `!isMasterDisabled && idAccount == idSigner` returns `tesSUCCESS` (`:1033-1036`) and a regular key is merely an additional accepted signer (`:1026-1030`). ⚠ **The path moved at this tag** — it is `src/libxrpl/tx/Transactor.cpp`, not the `src/xrpld/app/tx/detail/` path an older citation would use, which returns 404. `signer/src/ledger.rs:503-517` records the same reading and its line numbers still resolve. Either one **adds** an authorisation path. Neither closes the one that already exists. As custody controls, on their own, both are zero. ⚠ **What this paragraph did not say, added by `REVIEW-046` H4:** a regular key **with** `lsfDisableMaster` is accepted before the disabled master is considered (`Transactor.cpp:1027-1030`, then `:1039-1042`). Because movement also needs the ElGamal secret, a regular key and that secret held in independent custody domains already require two parties, with no signer list. And delegation is a fourth authorisation path that none of the three flags governs.

**`lsfDisableMaster` is the only flag that closes the master path, and the signer refuses to build anything for an account carrying it.** `signer/src/protocol.rs:121` and `:198`, `Refusal::MasterKeyDisabled`: *"This signer holds a family seed and signs as the account itself, so every transaction it could build for that account is dead on arrival. It will not build them. Not retryable."* So the one configuration that would actually split power 1 is, today, a hard incompatibility with the signer rather than a cost to be priced.

**And all three flip the keygen ruling's condition 2.** `AccountSecurity::master_seed_is_not_spending_authority` is true if any of `lsfDisableMaster`, a regular key, or a signer list is present (`ledger.rs:375-376`). For such an account the onboarding flips the **recommended** key source to `standalone` and makes the condition-2 warning mandatory (E-69, issue #11, discharged 2026-08-20). ⚠ **It does not force it, and an earlier draft of this request said it did.** `Assessment::recommended_source` is documented in those words — *"A recommendation, not a refusal. The ruling says warn and offer; it does not say refuse, and inventing a refusal would be inventing a rule"* (`signer/src/onboarding.rs:338-342`) — and `run` still accepts `derived-from-master-seed` for such an account behind a **second typed acknowledgement**, which `signer/tests/onboarding.rs:445-456` pins. So hardening movement authority **prices** the derived key at one more acknowledgement; it does not take it away.

**The shape of the decision, stated plainly.** ~~Splitting power 1 costs signer work on the signing path and a quorum that exceeds every individual weight.~~ ⚠ ~~It does NOT require giving up the derived key, and an earlier version of this very paragraph said it did — twice, in the two sentences that followed the ones correcting it. Under 2a, disabling the master key moves spending authority to the quorum while the derived seed stays a decryption credential, so power 1 and power 2 are split without giving up the derived key.~~ **Struck 2026-09-13 by `REVIEW-046` H2 and H4.** Splitting movement costs signer work either way, but not necessarily a quorum: a regular key with the master disabled also splits it, if the ElGamal secret is held elsewhere. And disabling the master key moves only the *signature* to the quorum. The derived seed remains a required input to every confidential spend, so under 2a its holder keeps a veto over movement as well as full read access. The derived key can be kept, but the seed's signing role is the only part of it that retires; it cannot be taken offline.

What neither variant does is **narrow** power 2: one credential still reads every movement this account is an endpoint of, and the issuer's secret and the facility book read every movement in the facility regardless (H3). ⚠ **That is a property of the single-account design rather than of XLS-96, and this request previously stated it as a protocol limit.** The protocol has no rotation and no recovery (P-10), so a leaked credential cannot be revoked and past movements cannot be re-hidden — but nothing forces the facility to use **one** Dedicated Account. Each account's `MPToken` carries its own `HolderEncryptionKey`, so partitioning the float across accounts bounds what a single Dedicated Account seed's leak reads, and nothing else's. That is Option 5, and it was missing.

## What is and is not at stake today

**The Devnet demo is not the concern, and pretending otherwise would inflate this.** The float is Devnet MPT with no value, ~~the seeds are in `/tmp/wconf/state.json` and `~/.wconf/state.json` under `0600`~~ ⚠ **(false, `REVIEW-046` M1: the Dedicated Account's seed is in `~/.wconf/facility-state.json`, mode `0644`, written with no mode by `run-facility.ts:106-107`, beside the bank's seed, the suppliers' seeds and the issuer's ElGamal secret; the directory is `0700` only because `tools/rebuild-env.sh:159` sets it, and `facility/src/signer.ts:113-116` leaves a copy in each signer's `profile.json` after the session ends — B-29)**, and `facility.ts:58` says outright that the seed is in `Party` *"because this process plays every role"*. Nothing here is a live custody incident.

**The design's claim is the concern.** `ADR-0002`'s comparison table at `:96` tells a reader that the Dedicated Account is *"an ordinary account with an ordinary key — whoever holds it can move the whole float"*, and rev 4's note at `:122` says that belongs in the weighing **before this ADR is accepted**. `ADR-0002` is still `Proposed`. So the open question is not what the demo does with a test seed; it is what the architecture says about a facility anyone would actually run, and whether that is written down honestly.

## The options

**Option 1 — accept it, by name, with a disclosure.** Extend the pattern `REVIEW-019` and E-151 already use: the risk is named, the disclosure is mandatory and printed, and no mechanism changes. Cost: a disclosure and a wording test on it. Buys: an honest document. Buys no protection whatsoever, and the disclosure has to say both powers ride on one key, including power 2, which is the half E-117 never stated. ⚠ **Since `REVIEW-046` it also has to say** that the same key is required to spend, that the issuer can erase and freeze the float, that the issuer's secret and the facility book read everything, and that losing the key locks the balance for good.

**Option 2 — split power 1. It has two variants, and an earlier draft of this request offered only the expensive one.** A signer list on the Dedicated Account plus `lsfDisableMaster`, and then a choice the onboarding leaves open:

- **2a — keep the derived key.** ~~Movement moves to the quorum; the (now disabled) master seed stays the decryption credential.~~ ⚠ **Corrected by `REVIEW-046` H2:** the signature moves to the quorum, and the disabled master seed stays both the decryption credential and a required input to every confidential spend. It cannot go offline, and its holder can veto any movement. Accepted behind the second typed acknowledgement described above. Its advantage is **no additional ElGamal secret** — precisely that, and no more. ⚠ **An earlier draft said *"no second secret comes into existence"*, which the paragraph below it contradicts**: both variants add signer keys, so the number of things held safely goes up either way. What 2a avoids is a second *decryption* credential.
- **2b — move to `elgamal_key_source: standalone`.** The onboarding's recommendation. ~~Movement sits with the quorum and decryption with a separate secret, so the two powers rest on different material~~ ⚠ **Corrected by `REVIEW-046` H2:** the signature sits with the quorum, and the standalone secret is needed both to decrypt and to prove every spend. So a movement needs the quorum and that secret's holder together — but that material is **a new long-lived secret somebody has to hold**, and it is as unrotatable as the one it replaces (P-10).

⚠⚠ **THE SPLIT IS A PROPERTY OF THE QUORUM, NOT OF HAVING A SIGNER LIST, and a list can be configured so that nothing is split at all.** XRPL accepts `SignerQuorum = 1`, and it accepts a member whose `SignerWeight` alone meets the quorum. Either way one key still moves the whole float and this option delivers nothing. **Two conditions, and the second is the one a quorum alone does not give.** The quorum must exceed every individual weight, *and* the signer keys must be held in **independent custody domains**. ⚠ **Two more, added by `REVIEW-046`:** no `Delegate` object may grant a movement permission (H4), and the ElGamal secret's custody has to be priced alongside the signers', because it is a required party to every movement (H2). ⚠ **A quorum alone requires several signatures, not several holders**: one operator holding every signer key still moves the whole float alone, and calling that a split would be claiming more security than is delivered, which `CLAUDE.md`'s standing prohibition forbids. Both conditions must be stated in the ruling, because neither flag implies either. ⚠ **The signer credentials are then themselves things to keep safe**, so 2a's advantage is narrower than *"nothing new to hold"*: it avoids an additional **ElGamal** secret, while both variants add signer keys.

Either variant makes *"whoever holds that key can move the whole float"* false **while keeping confidential positions**, under that quorum condition. ~~Option 4 makes it false more strongly, by having no key at all, and pays for that by giving up the premise.~~ ⚠ **Corrected by `REVIEW-046` M2:** under Option 4 the pseudo-account has no key, but a Vault's shareholders can withdraw, and a sole shareholder can withdraw the whole pool (`VaultWithdraw.cpp:211-226`). It pays for that by giving up the premise. ⚠ **And neither variant touches the issuer's power to erase or freeze the float** (H1). Cost, and it is the largest item here: the signer cannot sign for such an account at all today, so it must learn to sign as a signer-list member — new code on the signing path, which is a trust-boundary change and needs a full rotation before implementation, not after. The facility must also configure the list. ⚠ **Understated, `REVIEW-046` M4:** multisigning means a per-signer signing preimage, a `Signers` array, collecting and ordering signatures, and an owner reserve for the list (`SignerListSet.cpp:316-351`); today the signer emits one `SigningPubKey` and one `TxnSignature`.

**Option 3 — reduce what is at risk: hold no standing float.** Convert per-drawdown instead of once up front. ⚠ **Incomplete, `REVIEW-046` M3:** repayments route into the Dedicated Account and are merged (`facility.ts:445-446`, `:499-503`), so this alone still accumulates a float. Holding none also means converting back every repayment, which publishes each repayment size. Cost: it is a direct trade of confidentiality for custody. `ConfidentialMPTConvert` carries a plaintext `MPTAmount`, which is F-24's mechanism one step earlier, so converting per drawdown publishes the drawdown schedule and sizes. That breaks TASK ZERO §9's design rule — value enters confidentiality once, in aggregate — which is the premise the leak analysis rests on. Priced here because it is the only option that shrinks the exposure rather than splitting a key, and it should be visibly rejected rather than silently absent.

**Option 4 — take the other side of the axis: a `Vault` pseudo-account for the pooled leg, and no confidential positions on it.** This is the axis E-117 actually names — **confidential positions XOR non-custodial pooling, one or the other, today**. It is a reopening of `ADR-0002`, not a custody patch, and rev 4's blocker 1 (share tokens cannot be confidential) stands. Listed so the owner sees the whole axis rather than only the options inside one branch of it — **Options 1, 2, 3 and 5 all sit on the confidential-positions side**, and this one does not. ⚠ **The reversal condition is already recorded**: if `lsfMPTCanHoldConfidentialBalance` ever becomes settable on a `Vault` share issuance, this is re-argued.

**Option 5 — partition the float across several Dedicated Accounts.** Not a way to split a credential, but a way
to bound what one credential reads: a separate account per tranche, per epoch, or per supplier group, each with its
own `HolderEncryptionKey`. A leak of one seed then reads that partition and no other ⚠ **(of the Dedicated Account seeds only, `REVIEW-046` H3: the issuer's secret and a shared facility book read every partition)**, and rotation-by-replacement
becomes possible in the only sense XLS-96 allows — a **new account**, since keys themselves never rotate (P-10).
⚠ **It buys nothing about the past**: movements already made under a leaked key stay readable for ever.

Its costs are real and cut against the premise. Each partition needs its own Convert, and `ConfidentialMPTConvert`
carries a plaintext `MPTAmount`, so **the entry amounts become more granular the more partitions there are** —
F-24's mechanism, one step earlier. And the counterparty graph is public (P-11), so which supplier draws from which
partition is visible; ~~a per-supplier partition would publish exactly the grouping the facility exists to hide.~~ ⚠ **Wrong reason, `REVIEW-046` M6:** the facility hides amounts and nothing else (`facility.ts:30-34`, P-11), so the grouping is already public. A per-supplier partition is still costly, because its own Convert publishes that supplier's allocation, which is an amount.
**A per-epoch partition costs the least confidentiality and buys the least**, which is the trade to weigh. ⚠ **Unpriced, `REVIEW-046` M5:** each partition needs its own funding, key registration, signer profile and routing, and moving a partition out needs that partition's signing and proof credentials. After a loss it cannot be evacuated; after a leak, evacuation races the thief.

## What must not happen either way

**Do not describe the ElGamal secret as decryption-only, under any option.** It is a required party to every confidential spend (`REVIEW-046` H2), and a design that treats it as a read credential will put it somewhere a spend can be blocked or a loss cannot be recovered.

**Do not rule on the Dedicated Account's key without ruling on the issuer's.** The bank can erase and freeze the float without the Dedicated Account's key (`REVIEW-046` H1), so a ruling on one key alone leaves the float's custody unstated.

**Do not describe any outcome of this decision as non-custodial.** P-5 is a standing prohibition and Option 2 does not earn an exemption from it: an M-of-N signer list is custody by a committee, and the ElGamal secret remains a single unrotatable credential regardless. A facility whose float is movable by a quorum and readable by one key is not non-custodial, and saying so in a diagram caption is how the prohibition gets broken.

**Do not implement Option 2 without the rotation.** It changes what the signer will sign and for which account configurations. `CLAUDE.md`'s gate is explicit that this class of change gets the rotation before implementation.

**Do not fold this into the ADR by paraphrase.** If a ruling lands, `ADR-0002`'s custody row and its `:122` note are the sites to update, and E-117's live clause is the sentence that gets struck. Two documents describing one custody position is how E-126 happened.
